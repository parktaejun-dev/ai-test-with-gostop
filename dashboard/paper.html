Parameter Scale and Model-Family Effects in Fixed-Policy LLM Play for Four-Player Go-Stop

Go-Stop AI Evaluation Harness

Abstract: We evaluate fixed-policy large language model play in four-player Go-Stop, a Korean card game with stochastic deals, seat-order nuisance effects, multi-agent interaction, and risk-sensitive payoffs. The study asks whether performance within a Qwen panel improves monotonically with parameter scale, and whether model family still matters among roughly same-scale models served through NVIDIA Build. The Qwen result is non-monotonic: the 397B model leads mean profit and CVaR, while the larger 480B Coder model underperforms it. In the NVIDIA panel, Nemotron has the strongest mean-profit and tail-risk profile. The results support a constrained paper claim: Go-Stop policy quality is not explained by parameter count alone.

Research Questions

  1. RQ1. Within a Qwen panel, does Go-Stop performance improve monotonically with parameter scale?
  2. RQ2. Among roughly same-scale NVIDIA Build models, does model family affect Go-Stop performance?

RQ1 is answered negatively in this panel. RQ2 is answered positively under the constrained NVIDIA panel. These are panel-specific findings under fixed prompts, fixed seat permutations, short horizons, and remote-API budget constraints.

Limitations

The model panels are convenience samples from available remote APIs. The Qwen comparison mixes Coder and Qwen3.5 variants, so parameter scale is partly confounded with specialization. The NVIDIA run measures constrained first-decision behavior more than full-game autonomous play because only one remote call per agent was permitted in each session.
